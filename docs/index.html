Advaith Foundation

Education nonprofit · Bangalore, India

What they do

A charitable trust set up in 2004 that pays for the entire schooling, food and housing of children from very poor families in and around Bangalore, free to the family. Its Ankur programme puts those children into small residential homes and sends them to The Samhita Academy, a CBSE school off Bannerghatta Road, where they sit in the same classrooms as children whose parents pay fees; the money comes from donors and company CSR budgets, not from the children.

Inside, day to day. Teachers plan and mark lessons and run after-school tuition, house parents and wardens live with the children and track each one's attendance, health, marks and behaviour largely on paper, and a small office team writes the donor and CSR reports, keeps the trust's compliance filings in order, and handles the visits and paperwork that come with taking in a child.

Rostrum Education sells help getting into university: one-to-one admissions counselling for Ivy League, Oxbridge, boarding schools and medical or law school, plus subject tutoring for IB, A-Level, AP and IGCSE and prep for tests like the SAT, GMAT and UCAT. The customers are fee-paying families, mostly in the UAE, India, the UK and Hong Kong, who buy packages of hours with a counsellor and a tutor for a teenager applying abroad.

Inside, day to day. Counsellors sit on video calls with a student and their parents working through the same personal essay draft after draft, tutors teach scheduled lessons across four time zones, and a coordination team keeps track of which student is applying where, which deadline is next and which tutor is free at 7pm Dubai time.

Saint-Gobain Nordic and Baltic

Building materials, manufacturing and distribution · Nordic and Baltic region (Norway, Denmark, Sweden, Finland, Poland)

What they do

Saint-Gobain Nordic and Baltic is the regional arm of Saint-Gobain, a very large French company that manufactures building materials such as plasterboard, insulation, flat glass and plumbing products. In this region it also owns the builders' merchant chains that sell those materials, around 400 branches trading as Dahl, Brodrene Dahl, Optimera and Tadmar, so the buyers are construction firms, plumbers, installers and contractors picking up materials for a specific job.

Inside, day to day. Roughly 14,000 people: factory shifts making insulation and board, branch staff behind trade counters serving a contractor who walks in with a materials list, warehouse and delivery crews moving stock out of six central warehouses to 400 stores, and technical advisors answering a builder's question about which wall system to use where.

ShikshaLokam

Education nonprofit · Bengaluru, India

What they do

ShikshaLokam is a nonprofit, funded by the Shibulal family, that tries to improve India's government schools by supporting the people who run them rather than by running schools itself. It builds free open-source software and training programmes for head teachers and district and block education officers, and it convenes a movement called Shikshagraha aimed at reaching a million public schools; nobody pays for it, the money comes from philanthropy.

Inside, day to day. Programme staff spend their days on workshops and mentoring calls with head teachers and district officials, a product and engineering team builds and maintains the open-source platforms those officials use to observe schools and log improvements, and a lot of hours go into writing reports, newsletters, case stories and funder updates about what changed in which district.

Shiksharth

Education nonprofit, tribal India · Sukma, Bastar, Chhattisgarh, India

What they do

Shiksharth is a small trust, started in 2015, that works on schooling for tribal children in Sukma in south Bastar, a remote district shaped by decades of armed conflict. It works alongside the district government inside existing state schools on reading and numeracy, safe spaces and locally relevant lessons, reaching a reported 480 schools and 20,000-plus children; there are no paying customers, it is funded by philanthropies such as Wipro Foundation.

Inside, day to day. Around fifty-odd educators travel out to scattered village schools, run classroom sessions and teacher training in local tribal languages, and record attendance and reading levels largely on paper; a small product team designs the learning materials and kits, and a handful of operations people handle logistics, finances and the reports funders ask for.

Solinas Integrity

Deep tech, water robotics · Chennai, India

What they do

Solinas Integrity builds robots that go inside underground pipes and sewers so that people do not have to. It sells a camera-crawler robot for inspecting water and sewer lines, a robot for cleaning septic tanks and manholes, and a software platform that turns the footage into a condition report, and the buyers are city water boards and municipal corporations across India plus industrial customers like Suez, Veolia and vehicle plants.

Inside, day to day. Field crews drive to a city, open a manhole, lower a robot in and spend hours driving it through pipe; back in Chennai engineers watch that footage to mark every crack, blockage and joint failure and write it up as a report for the municipality, while robotics and software engineers build the next version and a business development team writes government tender bids.

Zerodha is India's largest stockbroker. Inside, day to day.
